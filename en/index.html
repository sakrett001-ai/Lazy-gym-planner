              About the demos

              The diagrams help break down the sequence of the movement. Range of motion depends on the exercise and the individual; the schematic silhouette does not prescribe required joint angles.

              Acknowledgements

              The mannequin’s bones come from the open model library MyoSim by the MyoSuite team (Apache 2.0 license). It is built on OpenSim research models: legs and pelvis — Rajagopal et al., 2016; arm, shoulder blade and hand — Holzbaur et al., 2005; lumbar spine — the lumbar spine model. Many thanks to the authors for sharing this data openly.

              The body shape in the Mannequin view is the open parametric human model Anny (NAVER; code under the Apache 2.0 license, MakeHuman mesh and weights under CC0), fitted to the mannequin’s joints: the skeleton, muscles and pose checks are unchanged.

              The hyoid bone, the laryngeal cartilages and the trachea are built to their sizes in adult men from anatomical studies: Fakhry et al., 2013; Rawal et al., 2014; Kim and Song, 2017; Gray’s Anatomy, 1918.

              Muscles (the “Anatomy” view) — paths from the same MyoSim models; shoulder-blade muscles — from the shoulder model DAS3 (Chadwick et al., 2014; Apache 2.0 license); pectineus — from the leg model Gait2392 (Delp et al., 1990; CC BY 3.0 license); neck muscles and missing muscle parts — from attachment descriptions in anatomy atlases. Muscle thickness follows their MRI volumes in 49 men (Riem et al., 2026), neck muscles — by MRI (Zheng et al., 2013), rescaled to the figure’s height and weight; leg tendon proportions — from cadaver specimens (Ward et al., 2009); intercostal muscles — as a sheet between the ribs, their thickness from ultrasound (Yoshida et al., 2019) and the layer masses in cadaver specimens (Wilson et al., 2001). As a muscle shortens it thickens: its volume stays the same.
